Earth Jukebox

English 中文 Open Earth Jukebox

How to use Earth Jukebox without seeing the screen

Earth Jukebox turns NASA's maps of the Earth into sound. Every place on Earth has a note. Warmer places play higher notes. You can hear how warm a place was in any year from 1880 to 2025.

The website is in English. The voice guide speaks English or Chinese. The numbers come from NASA.

Before you start

  • Use headphones if you can. The left and right of the Earth play in your left and right ear.
  • On an iPhone, turn off silent mode, or you will hear nothing.
  • If you use a screen reader (VoiceOver, TalkBack, NVDA or JAWS), keep Voice guide off. Your screen reader already reads everything. With both on, you would hear things twice.
  • If you don't use a screen reader, turn Voice guide on. It reads every step aloud, and whatever the mouse rests on (a button, any text, the reading, the notes you heard) it reads aloud.

What the sounds mean

  • A low note: colder than normal.
  • A middle note: normal. Normal means the average for 1951 to 1980.
  • A high note: much warmer than normal. When it is very warm, a second voice joins in.
  • A soft tick with no note: NASA has no data there.

You can hear this any time with the button What do the sounds mean?

The first screen

The page starts talking as soon as it opens. If you hear nothing (some phones, like iPhone, wait for a first tap), press any key or tap anywhere. You start on Start with voice guide, so pressing Enter starts the voice guide right away. The Tab key moves between the buttons and reads each one.

There are three buttons:

  • Start listening: choose this if you use a screen reader.
  • Start with voice guide: choose this if you don't use a screen reader. On a phone, double-tapping the top half of the first screen does the same.
  • 中文语音导览: the voice guide in Chinese. On a phone, double-tap the bottom half of the first screen.

After you start, a short tour shows what to do, one step at a time. Screen readers read each step. Press Skip or Escape to close it, or follow it.

What is on the page, from top to bottom

  1. Voice guide: a switch. It turns the spoken guide on or off.
  2. Controls: opens big buttons to move around the Earth.
  3. Three tabs: Play, My Earth song and Ask the Earth.
  4. Four tracks: Temperature, Ocean, Lightning and All three.
  5. Fly with the Space Station.
  6. The globe. A screen reader calls it "Temperature globe".
  7. Play 1880 → 2025. A screen reader may say "right arrow" between the years.
  8. The year bar (a slider).
  9. The Controls buttons, after you open them.
  10. Further down: the step-by-step guide, and More ways to listen.

On a phone (VoiceOver or TalkBack)

  1. Press Start listening.
  2. Go to the top of the page and press Controls. Big buttons open, and you land on Where am I?
  3. Press Where am I? It plays the note, then says the place, the year and how warm it was.
  4. Next to it are North, West, East and South. Each one moves you about 1,000 kilometres and says where you are now.
  5. Year before and Year after change the year by one.
  6. Add this place to my tune adds the place you are at to your tune. Add 3 places, and the guide lets you hear your tune in 1880 and in 2025.
  7. To hear the whole history of a place, press Play 1880 → 2025. It plays one note for each year from 1880 to 2025, in about 30 seconds, then says how much warmer it is than in the 1880s. Press it again to stop.
  8. To jump to a place, open More ways to listen, then press a place under Go to a place: Sibu, the Arctic Ocean, the Sahara, the Amazon rainforest, Siberia, the middle of the Pacific Ocean, Europe, central Australia or Antarctica.

You start at Sibu, Sarawak, in 2025.

Without a screen reader, double-tap anywhere on the first screen: the top half starts the voice guide in English, the bottom half in Chinese. (The first screen says this in both languages. On a computer, press Start with voice guide or 中文语音导览.) The screen then becomes a remote control that stays still, so you can learn where each button is. In the top half, Where am I? is in the middle, North is above it, South below, West on the left and East on the right. At the bottom are four buttons: Play and Next place, and under them Help: what can I do? and More. More opens a second screen: Back across the top, then What do the sounds mean?, Add this place to my tune, Year before, Year after, Slower, Faster, 中文 (or English) and Close. Tap a button once to hear what it does, and tap it again to press it. After each press, the guide suggests one new thing to try. Close puts the Earth back on the screen; Controls, at the top of the page, opens the remote again. If you get lost, tap anywhere with two fingers: the remote opens, or tells you again where everything is. Next time you open the page, one tap anywhere (or any key on a computer) starts the voice guide again.

Away from the remote, the phone works like a screen reader: tap anything once to hear it, and tap it again to press it. Touch and hold for a moment, then slide your finger, to hear everything under it; lift your finger, and a tap presses the last thing you heard. Swipe up or down to scroll.

On the Earth, with Voice guide on, you can listen by touch: slide one finger over the Earth and it keeps playing notes. Stop for a moment and it tells you where you are. A quick tap adds the place to your tune. Two fingers spin the Earth. A tick means your finger left the Earth.

On a computer (keyboard)

Move to the globe with the Tab key. Then use these keys:

  • Arrow keys: move around the Earth. Hold Shift for small steps.
  • Enter: say where I am and how warm it is.
  • Square brackets [ and ]: the year before and the year after.
  • Space: play every year from 1880 to 2025.
  • I: fly one orbit with the Space Station.
  • Numbers 1 to 9: go to a place. 1 is Sibu, 2 is the Arctic Ocean.
  • L: what do the sounds mean.
  • V: Voice guide on or off.
  • Minus and plus: the voice speaks slower or faster.
  • Question mark: hear all the keys.

With Voice guide on, you can also move the mouse over the Earth: it keeps playing notes, and when you stop for a moment it tells you where you are. A click adds the place to your tune.

With NVDA or JAWS, the globe should switch to focus mode by itself. If the arrow keys read text instead of moving, press NVDA plus Space (or Insert plus Z in JAWS) to switch to focus mode.

The four tracks

  • Temperature, played on a marimba: how much warmer or colder each place was than normal, every year since 1880.
  • Ocean, played on a harp: how warm the sea is. This is one day, 19 June 2007.
  • Lightning, played on drums: how often the Space Station saw lightning, 2017 to 2023. It only sees between about 56 degrees north and south, so places farther away stay silent.
  • All three: temperature, the nearest sea and lightning together, at the same place.

Fly with the Space Station

Press Fly with the Space Station. You fly one orbit, like the International Space Station, right over your place. It takes about 93 minutes in space and 15 seconds here. Every place below plays its note. At the end it tells you the warmest place it passed. Press Stop to stop early.

My Earth song

  1. Choose the tab My Earth song.
  2. In the box Tell your story, type a short story with places and years, in any language. For example: "My grandpa was born in Fuzhou in 1950. I was born in Kuching on 15 March 2006." Or press Speak and say it.
  3. Press Make my song from this, then Play my Earth song.

Ask the Earth

  1. Choose the tab Ask the Earth.
  2. Choose English or 中文 under the question box. Speak listens in that language, and the answer comes back in that language. If you type a question, the choice follows the language you typed, so the next Speak listens in it too.
  3. Type a question, in any language, or press Speak. For example: "Is Sibu warmer now than in 1960?"
  4. Press Ask. The answer plays as sound, then is read out.

No sound?

  • Turn up the volume. On an iPhone, turn off silent mode.
  • Press any button on the page once. Browsers only play sound after you press something.
  • If you are on a call on the same phone, the call can make the website quiet. Try a second device for the website.

Open Earth Jukebox

不看屏幕也能用 Earth Jukebox

Earth Jukebox 把 NASA 的地球地图变成声音。地球上每个地方都有一个音，越暖的地方，音越高。你可以听到一个地方从 1880 年到 2025 年每一年有多暖。

网站的文字是英文，但语音导览可以讲中文：按 中文语音导览，或在手机第一个画面的下半部快速点两下。之后随时可以按 中文 / English 切换。Ask the Earth（问地球）也可以用中文回答。数字都来自 NASA。

开始之前

  • 最好戴耳机。地球的左边和右边，会在左耳和右耳响。
  • 用 iPhone 的话，要关掉静音模式，不然听不到声音。
  • 有用读屏软件（VoiceOver、TalkBack、NVDA 或 JAWS）的话，Voice guide 请保持关闭。读屏软件已经会念，两个都开会念两次。
  • 没有用读屏软件的话，请打开 Voice guide，它会把每一步念出来；鼠标停在任何东西上（按钮、文字、读数、听过的音），它都会念出来。

声音代表什么

  • 低音：比正常冷。
  • 中音：正常。正常是指 1951 到 1980 年的平均温度。
  • 高音：比正常暖很多。很暖的时候，会多一个声音一起响。
  • 轻轻的"嗒"一声、没有音高：NASA 在那里没有资料。

随时可以按 What do the sounds mean? 再听一次。

第一个画面

网页一打开就会开始说话。如果没有听到（有些手机，例如 iPhone，要先点一下才会出声），按任何一个键或点一下页面。一开始的位置就在 Start with voice guide 上，直接按 Enter 就会开始语音导览。按 Tab 键可以在按钮之间移动，每到一个就念出来。

有三个按钮：

  • Start listening：有用读屏软件的，请按这个。
  • Start with voice guide：没有用读屏软件的，请按这个（英文语音）。用手机的话，在第一个画面的上半部快速点两下也可以。
  • 中文语音导览：用中文语音一步一步带你。用手机的话，在第一个画面的下半部快速点两下。

开始之后，会出现一个简短的新手引导，一次带你做一步。读屏软件会念出每一步。按 Skip 或 Esc 可以关掉，也可以照着做。

页面上从上到下有什么

  1. Voice guide：语音导览开关。
  2. Controls：打开一组大按钮，用来在地球上移动。
  3. 三个分页：Play、My Earth song、Ask the Earth。
  4. 四个曲目：Temperature（温度）、Ocean（海洋）、Lightning（闪电）、All three（三个一起）。
  5. Fly with the Space Station（跟着太空站飞一圈）。
  6. 地球。读屏软件会念 "Temperature globe"。
  7. Play 1880 → 2025。读屏软件可能会在两个年份中间念 "right arrow"。
  8. 年份条（滑杆）。
  9. 打开 Controls 之后的大按钮。
  10. 再往下：一步一步的导览，还有 More ways to listen。

用手机（VoiceOver 或 TalkBack）

  1. 按 Start listening。
  2. 到页面最上面，按 Controls。大按钮会打开，焦点会停在 Where am I?
  3. 按 Where am I?：先弹出那个地方的音，再念出地点、年份和有多暖。
  4. 旁边有 North（北）、West（西）、East（东）、South（南）。每按一次移动大约 1,000 公里，并念出你现在在哪里。
  5. Year before 和 Year after：前一年、后一年。
  6. Add this place to my tune：把你现在的位置加进你的旋律。加满 3 个地方，导览就会让你听这段旋律在 1880 年和 2025 年的样子。
  7. 想听一个地方的完整历史，按 Play 1880 → 2025。它会用大约 30 秒，把 1880 到 2025 年一年一个音弹一遍，最后说出这里比 1880 年代暖了多少。再按一次可以停。
  8. 想直接去某个地方，打开 More ways to listen，在 Go to a place 下面选：诗巫、北冰洋、撒哈拉、亚马逊雨林、西伯利亚、太平洋中间、欧洲、澳洲中部、南极。

一开始的位置是 2025 年的诗巫（Sibu, Sarawak）。

没有用读屏软件的话，在第一个画面快速点两下：点上半部是英文语音导览，点下半部是中文语音导览。（第一个画面会用英文和中文说明。用电脑的话，按 Start with voice guide 或 中文语音导览。）接着整个屏幕会变成一个不会卷动的遥控器，摸几次就记得每个按钮在哪里。上半部：中间是"我在哪里"（Where am I?），上面是北，下面是南，左边是西，右边是东。最下面有四个按钮："播放"和"下一个地方"，再下面是"帮助"和"更多"。"更多"会打开第二个画面：最上面是"返回"，下面是"声音代表什么"、"把这里加进旋律"、"前一年"、"后一年"、"慢一点"、"快一点"、"英文"（或"中文"）和"关闭"。按钮点一下，会念出它是做什么的；再点一下，才是按下去。每按一次，导览会建议一件还没试过的事。按"关闭"会回到地球；要再打开遥控器，按页面最上面的 Controls。迷路的话，用两根手指在任何地方点一下：遥控器会打开，或者再讲一次每个按钮在哪里。下次再打开网页时，在任何地方点一下（电脑上按任何键），语音导览就会再打开。

遥控器以外的地方，手机像读屏软件一样：任何东西点一下，就会念出来；再点一下，才是按下去。按住不动一下，再滑动手指，手指经过的东西都会念出来；放开手指后，点一下就会按下最后听到的那个。上下滑是卷动页面。

开着 Voice guide 的话，在地球上也可以用摸的：一根手指在地球上滑，它会一路弹出音；停一下，它会念出你在哪里。快速点一下，会把这个地方加进旋律。两根手指是转地球。听到"嗒"一声，表示手指滑出地球了。

用电脑（键盘）

用 Tab 键移到地球，然后用这些键：

  • 方向键：在地球上移动。按住 Shift 是小步。
  • Enter：说出我在哪里、有多暖。
  • 方括号 [ 和 ]：前一年、后一年。
  • 空白键：把 1880 到 2025 年一年一个音播一遍。
  • I：跟着太空站飞一圈。
  • 数字 1 到 9：去某个地方。1 是诗巫，2 是北冰洋。
  • L：声音代表什么。
  • V：语音导览开或关。
  • 减号和加号：讲慢一点、快一点。
  • 问号：念出所有按键。

开着 Voice guide 的话，也可以用鼠标在地球上移动：它会一路弹出音，停一下就念出你在哪里。点一下会把这个地方加进旋律。

用 NVDA 或 JAWS 的话，到了地球应该会自动切换到焦点模式。如果按方向键是在读文字、没有移动，请按 NVDA 加空白键（JAWS 是 Insert 加 Z）切换到焦点模式。

四个曲目

  • Temperature，木琴：每个地方比正常暖或冷多少，1880 年起每一年。
  • Ocean，竖琴：海水有多暖。这是 2007 年 6 月 19 日这一天。
  • Lightning，鼓：太空站多常看到闪电，2017 到 2023 年。它只看得到大约北纬 56 度到南纬 56 度，更北或更南的地方不会出声。
  • All three：同一个地方，温度、最近的海水和闪电一起响。

跟着太空站飞一圈

按 Fly with the Space Station。你会像国际太空站一样绕地球飞一圈，正好经过你所在的地方。在太空中要大约 93 分钟，这里 15 秒。经过的每个地方都会弹出它的音，最后告诉你经过最暖的地方是哪里。想提早停，按 Stop。

My Earth song（我的地球之歌）

  1. 选分页 My Earth song。
  2. 在 Tell your story 的格子里，打一小段有地方和年份的故事，什么语言都可以。例如："我爷爷 1950 年在福州出生。我 2006 年 3 月 15 日在古晋出生。" 也可以按 Speak 用说的。
  3. 按 Make my song from this，再按 Play my Earth song。

Ask the Earth（问地球）

  1. 选分页 Ask the Earth。
  2. 在问题格子下面选 English 或 中文。按 Speak 时会听那个语言，答案也会用那个语言写出来、念出来。用中文打字问，答案会自动用中文，切换键也会跟着跳到中文，下一次按 Speak 就会听华语。
  3. 打一个问题，什么语言都可以，或者按 Speak 用说的。例如："诗巫现在比 1960 年暖吗？"
  4. 按 Ask。答案会先用声音弹出来，再念出来。

没有声音？

  • 把音量调大。iPhone 要关掉静音模式。
  • 在页面上随便按一个按钮。浏览器要你先按一下，才会出声。
  • 如果同一支手机正在通话，通话可能会让网站变小声。试试用另一台装置开网站。
