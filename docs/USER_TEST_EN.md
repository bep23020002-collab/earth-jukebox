# Earth Jukebox: online test script (English)

Read the lines in quotes out loud. Everything else is a note for the host.
About 20 minutes per person. One person hosts; a second person (if you have one) fills in the record sheet and notes the times.

- Website: https://bep23020002-collab.github.io/earth-jukebox/
- Guide for the tester: https://bep23020002-collab.github.io/earth-jukebox/guide.html

**Rules for the host**
- Don't help unless the tester has been stuck for about a minute. Then tell them only the name of the button, and write "needed a hint" on the sheet.
- Never say "right" or "wrong". Just say "Thank you" and move on.
- If the tester wants to stop, stop.

---

## Message to send before the call
> "Hi! We're in a NASA competition and made a website that turns Earth's temperature into sound, for people who can't see maps. Could you try it with us online for about 20 minutes? We are testing the website, not you. There are no wrong answers, and you can stop at any time.
> Please have headphones, and if possible two devices: one for the call and one for the website.
> Website: https://bep23020002-collab.github.io/earth-jukebox/
> How to use it: https://bep23020002-collab.github.io/earth-jukebox/guide.html
> Please use your screen reader as usual."

---

## Step 0. Welcome and consent (3 minutes)
> "Thank you so much for helping us. We are a student team from Sibu, Sarawak, in the NASA Space Apps Challenge. We made a website that turns NASA's Earth maps into sound, so people can explore them without seeing the screen."
> "Today we are testing the website, not you. There are no right or wrong answers. If something is confusing, that's the website's fault, and it helps us fix it. You can stop at any time."

Ask each question and write the answer on the sheet:
> "Are you 18 or older?"
> "May we record this call? We will only use it to improve the website and to report our results."
> "If we quote something you say, should we use your name, or keep you anonymous?"
> "Can we put about 3 seconds of you trying it in our competition video? It will be public on YouTube. Should we show your name?"

> "How would you describe your vision, in your own words?"
> "What device are you using today, and which screen reader, if any?"
> "How often do you use it?"

If they don't agree to recording or the video, don't record. Just do the test.

## Step 1. Open the website (2 minutes)
> "Please open the website link I sent you."
> "You'll find two buttons: Start listening, and Start with voice guide."

- Screen reader user:
  > "Please press Start listening. Please keep Voice guide off, because your screen reader already reads everything."
- No screen reader:
  > "Please press Start with voice guide."

> "Can you hear any sound? Please tell me what you hear."

## Step 2. Learn the sounds (3 minutes)
> "At the very top of the page there is a button called Controls. Please find it and press it."
> "Now please find the button What do the sounds mean? and press it. Listen to the three notes."

> "What does a high note mean?"
(Expected: warmer than normal. Write down their exact words.)

## Step 3. Task A: Which place is warmer? (4 minutes)
> "Please press Where am I?"
(It says Sibu, the year 2025, and how warm it was.)
> "Now press North a few times. Each press moves you about 1,000 kilometres."
> "Where are you now, roughly? Is it warmer or colder than normal there?"

> "Next, please find More ways to listen, open it, and under Go to a place, press Arctic Ocean. Listen. Then press Sibu, Sarawak. Listen."
(On a computer they can also press the keys 2 and 1.)
> "Of these two places, which one is much warmer than normal?"
(Correct answer: the Arctic Ocean. Note whether they hesitated.)

If they agreed to the video, this is the first moment to note the time.

## Step 4. Task B: Has it got warmer? (3 minutes)
> "Please go back to Sibu, and press Play 1880 → 2025. It plays every month from 1880 to 2025 in about 30 seconds. Just listen."
(On a computer they can press the Space bar.)
> "After listening, do you think it got warmer, colder, or stayed about the same here?"
(Correct answer: warmer. Write down the words they use, for example "higher", "brighter".)

## Step 5. Task C: Free play (2 minutes, optional)
> "Now you can explore freely for two minutes. You could try Fly with the Space Station, or open My Earth song and type a short story with a place and a year, like the town where you were born."
> "Please tell me what you're thinking as you go."

## Step 6. Last questions (3 minutes)
> "On a scale from 1 to 5, how easy was it to use? 1 is very hard, 5 is very easy."
> "Was anything confusing, or is there anything you would change?"
> "Would you show this to a friend? Why or why not?"
> "Could you describe this website in one sentence?"

If they agreed to the video:
> "Could you say that once more, a bit slower? We'd like to record it clearly."

## Closing
> "Thank you very much. Your feedback really helps. If we change something because of what you said, we'll let you know."

**If someone gets stuck** (can't find a button, the screen reader doesn't read something, no sound): note the step, the device and the screen reader, and send them to us right away. We'll fix it the same day.

## How to report the results (copy into the submission, with real numbers)
> "We tested Earth Jukebox online with N people who are blind or have low vision, on their own phones with their screen readers: X of N found the warmer of two places by sound alone, and X of N heard that Sibu has warmed since 1880. One said: '…'."

Report the real numbers, even if they're small. Only quote people who agreed.
